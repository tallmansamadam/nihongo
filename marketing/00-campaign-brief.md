## The campaign in one paragraph

Position Nihongo Reader as a **reading environment for serious Japanese learners**
— not a course, not a game. The promise: **"Real Japanese, fully explained — no
streaks, no ads, no account. Just reading."** Lead creative concept and tagline:
**"Every kanji, all the way down."** — the recursive grapheme drill-down is both
the differentiator and the most demoable feature (a kanji decomposing on screen
IS the ad). Launch to the open-source crowd first (they'll sideload), then widen
to plateaued-app learners and JLPT candidates as the web demo and Play Store
listing remove friction.

## The Duolingo question — decided

The team unanimously landed on: **keep the plateau, drop the anti-AI.**

- ✅ **Use:** "Finished your streak and still can't read a menu?" — opinion-framed,
  flatters the learner, matches a widely documented sentiment. At most one such
  line per piece; the enemy is *shallow gamification*, never a company's people.
- ❌ **Don't use:** "Tired of their AI adoption." This repo is public and visibly
  AI-assisted — the angle would be falsified within hours by exactly the
  audiences we're courting (HN, Reddit). Instead, **counter-position on
  transparency**: disclose the AI-assisted tooling in the README/FAQ proactively,
  and let "no ads, no account, no streaks, offline, open source" — every word
  verifiable — carry the contrast.

## Audiences (in launch order)

1. **Open-Source Tinkerer** — HN/GitHub/F-Droid crowd; the only segment that
   installs an APK without friction. Sells the *how* (KanjiVG decomposition, one
   codebase → desktop/Android/web).
2. **Plateau Graduate** — "streak-rich, reading-poor." Core emotional target.
3. **JLPT Candidate** — practice tests + kanji depth; always say "JLPT-*style*,
   unofficial."
4. **Anime-to-Manga Graduate** — song study packs + real-text reading as the
   bridge from subtitles.

*(Japan-bound travelers were demoted from audience to aesthetic — the retro
travel-ad look sells the dream, but travelers don't convert to daily study.)*

## Prerequisites before ANY promotion (Phase 0, ~2–4 weeks)

1. **Hosted web demo (GitHub Pages)** — the single most important unlock; the
   app is already a Vite build, so this is near-zero work. No channel converts
   without a one-click try link. Also becomes the SEO surface.
2. **Play Store listing** — start immediately; closed-testing requirements add
   weeks of calendar time.
3. Demo GIF + screenshots; README/FAQ transparency section; one-tap error
   reporting for content credibility.

## Launch sequence

- **Phase 1 (launch week):** Show HN (open-source dev story) → Product Hunt →
  r/LearnJapanese teaching-first post (after warming up in daily threads and
  getting mod clearance).
- **Phase 2 (sustain, ~4–5 hrs/week solo):** 2–3 templated kanji-breakdown
  Shorts/TikToks per week (every kanji in the app is a 30-second video), one
  useful community post, steady per-kanji SEO pages on the web demo.

## Measurement (solo-trackable)

GitHub stars · web-demo sessions · APK/store installs · Reddit/HN post karma &
comment sentiment · returning-visitor rate on the demo.

## Copy kit highlights (see 03)

- Tagline: **"Every kanji, all the way down."**
- Hero: headline + subhead + CTA in brand voice, ready for the landing page.
- 6 persona-matched ad variants (≤40 words), 3 short-video scripts (including
  the kanji-decomposition loop and a retro travel-ad spot reusing the splash
  aesthetic), creative direction for a unified retro look, and an honest
  launch-story post for Reddit/HN.

## Top risks

1. **AI-hypocrisy blowback** → angle dropped; proactive disclosure.
2. **Distribution friction** (sideload-only today) → web demo + store listings
   are Phase 0, not afterthoughts.
3. **Content credibility** (solo-authored Japanese) → "JLPT-style" phrasing,
   error reporting, native-speaker review pass before pushing the JLPT pillar.
